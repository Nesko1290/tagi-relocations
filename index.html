A word from Maureen

Moving to Geneva is a big step. I have taken it too.

I was born in Kenya and came to Switzerland at 25 for my first diplomatic posting — first in Zurich, then in Geneva. After my years as a Kenyan diplomat, I joined the United Nations at UNHCR, where I worked until 2024. I became Swiss along the way. I know what it means to arrive with a new post, a family to settle and a city that does not open its doors easily.

Over the years, colleagues kept asking me the same question: where should we live, and how do we get an apartment? Then expat families from outside the UN started asking too. Helping them became my calling. Today I am retired, I live in Versoix, and I give each search the care I would give my own family.

Tagi Relocations is our family business. My family works alongside me, so you are never passed from desk to desk: you deal with people who know your name and your story. We will listen, tell you honestly what is realistic, and stay with you until you hold your keys — and after.

    Questions

    What newcomers ask first.

    Can you start before I arrive in Geneva?

    Yes, and it is usually the best moment. Maureen searches, visits on your behalf on live video and prepares your application, so you can sign soon after you arrive.

    We have children. Can you find something near their school?

    It is one of Maureen's specialities. Many families choose Versoix, Genthod or Bellevue for the Collège du Léman and the other schools of the north shore, or Grand-Saconnex for the Campus des Nations. Tell us the school and we search around it.

    I am new to Switzerland, with no Swiss bank account or debt record yet. Is that a problem?

    Not with the right file. Expats usually rent with a work contract and a permit (or a permit in progress); international civil servants hold a carte de légitimation instead. In both cases agencies accept an employer attestation instead of local payslips. Maureen knows what each agency expects.

    How long does a search take?

    It depends on budget, timing and flexibility on location, but most searches conclude within a few weeks. You get a realistic estimate on the first call.

    Can you also help with insurance and a bank account?

    Yes. Maureen explains which insurance you need — Swiss health insurance, or your employer's or UN plan — and introduces you to trusted advisors, and she connects you with banks used to working with expats and international staff — including the blocked account for your rental deposit.

    How much deposit will I need?

    Swiss law caps the rental deposit at three months' rent, held in a blocked bank account in your name. Rental guarantee insurance is an alternative — we explain both.

    What does the service cost?

    It depends on the services you need. After the free first call you receive a clear written quote. Some employers reimburse relocation support; we provide invoices in the format your HR needs.
